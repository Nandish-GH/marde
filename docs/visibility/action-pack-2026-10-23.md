### r/ems — practical receiving constraints

Title: What makes an outdoor equipment handoff impractical before EMS arrival?

Disclosure: I am building MARDE Inc., a New Jersey pre-prototype emergency robotics startup. We have no deployed system or announced commercial pilot. I am trying to understand requirements, not advertise equipment for sale.

Our proposed approach would bring training supplies to a cleared outdoor receiving area using an operator-directed ground platform after aerial transport. The hard question is whether anyone can actually receive the equipment without creating more work or distraction.

For practitioners willing to discuss general scenarios: what would make this handoff useless or unsafe? An unavailable receiver, a locked gate, unclear responsibility or loss of communication are examples we want to account for. Please avoid patient details. I am especially interested in reasons a team should stop or reject the concept, rather than assume a faster arrival is automatically useful.

### r/drones — deployment versus recovery

Title: Evaluating recovery separately from payload deployment in an air–ground concept

Disclosure: I am the founder of MARDE Inc., a New Jersey pre-prototype team. We have no completed integrated demonstration or deployed system. This is a design question, not a flight-test result.

Our proposed sequence lowers a small ground platform from hover, but recovers it at a separate open rendezvous after the aircraft lands. We are treating those as different transitions with different failure paths. Before any aircraft work, the plan needs to establish mass, interfaces and fixed-rig handling under controlled conditions.

What information would you insist on seeing before even considering an integrated demonstration? I would value critique of the assumptions around touchdown confirmation, release and manually assisted recovery. We are not looking for ways around aviation permissions, and I cannot offer measured flight performance.
